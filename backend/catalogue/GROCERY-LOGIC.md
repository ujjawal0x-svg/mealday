# Ingredient identities, grocery packs and budget limits

`src/ingredientIdentity.ts` uses reviewed, exact aliases plus compatible mass/volume conversions. It preserves cooked vs raw rice, whole-wheat vs all-purpose flour, butter vs ghee, spice seeds vs powder, different oils and ambiguous names. Original recipe ingredient names/amounts and database records are unchanged. Canonical shopping lines use one anchored catalogue pack definition, rather than adding conflicting importer pack estimates as separate purchases. A conservative 50-g-to-one-whole-egg conversion follows the existing importer's quantity contract; it is not a universal egg size claim.

`basket` aggregates canonical household quantities before pantry deduction and rounding to whole packs. It computes pack costs in integer cents and uses micro-unit boundaries to avoid floating-point extra packs. It rejects unavailable ingredients, missing recipes and invalid packs/prices instead of assuming zero cost. Unknown pantry keys are retained until their catalogue is available, and are never deducted from unrelated ingredients. Pantry editing replaces the combined canonical quantity, so legacy alias entries cannot keep reappearing and double-counting. Dislikes and ingredient-at-home matching use the same identities.

Legacy kitchens migrate pantry entries and preferences without changing recipe units. Bought ticks migrate conservatively: a merged line remains bought only when every original required line was marked bought. A stored schema version avoids repeating the migration; older cloud snapshots explicitly retain the older version until migrated.

Full-day generation first tries its variety plan. If the complete estimated pack basket exceeds the budget, a bounded beam search retains both varied and cheaper alternatives. Costs account for shared packs and pantry stock across the entire plan. It may repeat otherwise eligible meals to fit the budget, and the existing variety notice explains this. Cuisine, diet, allergies, equipment and time never relax. A successful plan is checked again against the budget in cents before return. Search is bounded, so failure means no plan was found by this search, not proof of mathematical infeasibility or a claimed minimum spend. Failed builds preserve the saved plan and history.

Manual changes that increase an over-budget estimate are blocked. Existing saved plans can become over budget after household, budget or pantry changes; they are labelled and the user can rebuild. Cost-reducing changes to such a saved plan remain allowed. Grocery estimates exclude delivery/service fees and are not live retailer quotes. The canonical planner is the current full-day client workflow; existing provider/legacy API results are checked before the UI accepts them.

Checks: `node scripts/test-grocery-budget.cjs`, the catalogue/Kaggle/Emirati regression suites and the production build. Fixtures verify alias aggregation, pantry conservation and replacement, unit conversion, dislikes, non-equivalent forms, decimal pack boundaries, excluded meal modes, real-catalogue weekly budgets, failed budgets and exhaustive feasibility on a tiny catalogue. Previous variety tests use a generous budget to isolate variety from the new hard spending constraint.
